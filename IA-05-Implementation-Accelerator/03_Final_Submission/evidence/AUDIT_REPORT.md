## What could not be verified in this environment

Four limits, all of which the human should close manually before relying on this audit.

**First and most important: approval persistence across a page reload was never observed.** The built-in preview pane serves the page from a `data:` URL, where `window.localStorage` throws `Storage is disabled inside 'data:' URLs`. I confirmed the app's `try/catch` absorbs this cleanly and that in-session approvals work, but every claim about state *surviving a reload* — including the reviewer README's "approvals are held in this browser only" and my A-04 finding about stale approvals outliving content edits — rests on **reading `lsGet`/`lsSet`/`restore()` in source, not on watching it happen**. Someone should open `03_Final_Submission/artifact/app.html` by double-clicking it in a real Chrome, Edge and Firefox, approve a module, reload, and confirm both that the approval returns and that it is scoped to that browser. **Second**, the rendered DOM of a data-load failure (A-06) could not be observed: the pane refuses to execute JS for files outside the project folder, so I evidenced the `JSON.parse` throw in Node and traced the consequence through source rather than seeing the blank page. **Third**, everything here was observed in the pane's single Chromium-based engine at two viewport sizes; there is no cross-browser, cross-OS, print, zoom, or real-screen-reader evidence, and the A-09 accessibility findings are structural observations rather than the output of an assistive-technology session. **Fourth**, no human or SME judgement was applied to content accuracy: I verified that every citation resolves to a document, slide or section that exists and says what the module claims, and that each `best` answer is the one its cited SOP section supports — but confirming that these nine modules describe Professional Services practice *correctly and completely* is exactly what the 8 open SME questions exist for, and no part of this audit substitutes for that. Separately, note that A-01, A-02 and A-04 are the three findings that matter for a judged demo: the first two can make the Dependency Planner state something false about a real order, and the third weakens the governance control the package presents as its safety story.
